# VANTAGE | Hybrid Heavy Duty Maintenance Programme

**Programme ID:** `VANTAGE-HDM-AB-LISS-2026-10`  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 25 October 2026  
**Status:** Proposed — ready to load and personalise in VANTAGE Coach / VANTAGE App  
**Phase:** Maintenance  
**Duration:** Initial eight-week experiment; reassess before extending  
**Rotation:** Two weeks, repeated four times  
**Structure:** Three resistance sessions and four planned LISS sessions per week (Sunday LISS may be replaced with full rest)

## 1. Purpose and coaching intent

Combine the useful parts of Mike Mentzer's earlier **Evolved Heavy Duty** approach—high-quality working sets, controlled execution, progressive overload and recovery—with a modern, moderate-volume resistance programme. Maintain existing strength and lean mass without chasing fatigue or failure on every set.

This is **not** a deload, an aggressive hypertrophy block, or Mentzer's later ultra-minimalist consolidated routine.

### Primary objectives

1. Maintain current lean mass and exercise performance.
2. Reduce unnecessary accumulated fatigue compared with a higher-volume bodybuilding split.
3. Maintain cardiovascular fitness and routine through low-intensity steady-state work (LISS).
4. Protect shoulder function by avoiding loaded patterns that reproduce shoulder pain.
5. Test the response over eight weeks using comparable exercise histories and VANTAGE recovery data.

### Exercise identity rule

When analysing progression, compare only the **same exercise and machine variation**. In particular, **Iso-Lateral Bench Press** and **Hammer Strength Iso Lateral Decline Plate Press** are different movements and must never be merged into a single progression series.

## 3. Resistance session A — Legs, Chest & Triceps

**Session ID:** `HDM-A`  
**Working sets:** 13–14 total (warm-ups excluded)  
**Estimated duration:** 45–55 minutes  
**Default intensity:** 1–2 reps in reserve (RIR) on compound movements. Isolation sets generally 1–2 RIR, with an occasional final set at 0–1 RIR if technique and symptoms permit.

| Order | Exercise ID | Exercise | Working sets | Target reps | Rest | Key execution note |
|---:|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | `A-01` | Leg press | 2 | 8–12 | 2–3 min | Full comfortable range; controlled descent; no bouncing |
| 2 | `A-02` | Seated leg curl | 2 | 8–12 | 60–120 sec | Stable hips; controlled eccentric |
| 3 | `A-03` | Leg extension | 1–2 | 10–15 | 60–120 sec | Avoid swinging; optional last set near technical failure |
| 4 | `A-04` | Standing calf raise | 2 | 10–15 | 60–120 sec | Full comfortable stretch and pause |
| 5 | `A-05` | Incline machine chest press, neutral grip | 2 | 6–10 | 2–3 min | Only if shoulder pain-free; stable movement path |
| 6 | `A-06` | Pec deck **or** cable fly | 2 | 10–15 | 60–120 sec | Use whichever is comfortable for the shoulder |
| 7 | `A-07` | Rope triceps pushdown | 2 | 8–12 | 60–120 sec | Control elbow position and avoid shoulder irritation |

**Priority alternation:** On every second `HDM-A` session, move chest work (`A-05` and `A-06`) ahead of leg work, to avoid permanently training the chest after leg fatigue. Keep the same exercises, sets and targets. Record the order used so performance comparisons account for it.

**Coach note:** Do not prescribe forced repetitions, forced negatives or painful stretching. Warm-ups are additional, light-to-moderate preparation sets and are **not counted** in the working-set totals.

### A-session substitutions

- `A-05` incline machine press → a **pain-free** neutral-grip chest press variation; do not substitute a painful movement simply to retain a planned exercise.
- `A-06` pec deck ↔ cable fly depending on comfort and range of motion.
- `A-01` leg press → stable machine squat variation if the leg press is unavailable and the replacement is tolerated.
- Record any replacement as a **separate exercise identity** with its own progression history.

## 4. Resistance session B — Back, Shoulders & Biceps

**Session ID:** `HDM-B`  
**Working sets:** 12 total (warm-ups excluded)  
**Estimated duration:** 40–50 minutes  
**Default intensity:** 1–2 RIR for compound movements. Last isolation sets may approach technical failure selectively, only if comfortable.

| Order | Exercise ID | Exercise | Working sets | Target reps | Rest | Key execution note |
|---:|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | `B-01` | Neutral-grip lat pulldown | 2 | 6–10 | 2–3 min | Drive elbows down; no swinging |
| 2 | `B-02` | Chest-supported row | 2 | 8–12 | 2–3 min | Keep torso supported; controlled return |
| 3 | `B-03` | Machine pullover | 1 | 10–12 | 60–120 sec | Stay in a comfortable shoulder range |
| 4 | `B-04` | Cable lateral raise | 2 | 12–20 | 60–120 sec | Control the arc; no shrugging or swinging |
| 5 | `B-05` | Reverse pec deck | 2 | 12–20 | 60–120 sec | Emphasise rear delts and control |
| 6 | `B-06` | Cable biceps curl | 2 | 8–12 | 60–120 sec | Stable upper arm; full comfortable range |
| 7 | `B-07` | Hammer curl | 1 | 10–12 | 60–120 sec | Controlled reps; neutral wrist |

**Shoulder rule:** Heavy overhead pressing, weighted dips and upright rows are deliberately **not included** at baseline because shoulder pain has been reported under load. This is a practical modification, not a diagnosis.

**Coach note:** The machine pullover is a lat-focused accessory, not a forced substitute for a painful movement. Stop or modify exercises that reproduce shoulder pain.

### B-session substitutions

- `B-01` lat pulldown → alternate pain-free pulldown grip or assisted pull-up variation.
- `B-02` chest-supported row → chest-supported incline-bench dumbbell row.
- `B-03` machine pullover → pain-free straight-arm cable pulldown if unavailable.
- Keep substitution histories separate rather than combining unlike movements in progress reports.

## 5. LISS and non-lifting day protocol

**Intensity:** Conversational effort; approximately RPE **3–4/10** on main LISS days and **2–3/10** on Sunday. Do not force a fixed percentage-of-max-heart-rate zone, especially when individual resting heart rate is unusually low or medication/other factors may affect heart rate.

| Session ID | Day | Primary modality | Duration | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `HDM-LISS-TUE` | Tuesday | Incline treadmill walk | 30–40 min | RPE 3–4 | Choose a comfortable grade after Monday lifting; avoid residual leg soreness from overdoing the incline |
| `HDM-LISS-THU` | Thursday | Stationary bike or easy walk | 35–45 min | RPE 3–4 | Select modality that leaves legs recovered for Friday |
| `HDM-LISS-SAT` | Saturday | Outdoor walk | 30–40 min | RPE 3–4 | Prefer flatter or rolling terrain; no intervals |
| `HDM-LISS-SUN` | Sunday | Easy recovery walk **or rest** | 20–30 min if performed | RPE 2–3 | Optional; if run-down or sore, record a full rest day instead |

**LISS objective:** Maintain cardiovascular activity with low fatigue. These are **not** threshold, HIIT, tempo or interval sessions. Log actual minutes, modality, incline/speed (if relevant), RPE and notes. Do not convert an easy day into another high-effort workout.

## 6. Global training rules

1. **Warm up first.** Perform 5–10 minutes of easy movement plus exercise-specific ramp-up sets as required before challenging lifts. Ramp-up/warm-up sets are distinct from working sets.
2. **Working sets count:** `HDM-A` = 13–14; `HDM-B` = 12. A 1–2-set entry means only the listed working sets, not warm-up sets.
3. **Compounds:** Finish most sets with **1–2 RIR** (repetitions you could still complete with sound form).
4. **Isolations:** Usually maintain **1–2 RIR**; occasionally take the last set to **0–1 RIR** (technical, not forced failure) if pain-free and well-recovered.
5. **Rest:** Compounds **2–3 minutes**; isolations **60–120 seconds**. Rest longer if needed to maintain quality.
6. **Execution:** Full comfortable range of motion, controlled eccentric, reproducible technique. Never trade form for an extra rep.
7. **Progression:** Use a double-progression model. Build reps toward the top of the range across all working sets at the target RIR; then add the smallest sensible load increment and build again.
8. **No automatic load increases:** If reps, range of motion or symptoms deteriorate, retain or reduce load rather than forcing progression.
9. **Failure techniques:** Forced reps, heavy assisted negatives and high-fatigue finishers are not default programme elements.
10. **Recovery:** At least one non-lifting day between scheduled resistance sessions. Sunday walking is optional and may become complete rest.
11. **Shoulder pain:** Do not train through sharp, increasing or persistent shoulder pain. Change the movement or stop. If pain persists or limits function, seek an appropriate clinical assessment.
12. **Session order:** Compare similar exercises under similar conditions; note A-session chest-first/legs-first variation explicitly.

### Double-progression example

For `A-05` at 2 × 6–10 reps: when both working sets reach **10 good reps** at the target RIR and without shoulder symptoms, increase load by the smallest practical increment at the next comparable exposure. After increasing load, repetitions can fall back toward the lower end of the range.

## 7. Eight-week review structure

| Weeks | Focus | Coach checks |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Establish baselines and tolerable loads | Current reps, RIR, technique, shoulder symptoms, LISS tolerance, session fatigue |
| 3–4 | Increase reps where appropriate | Comparable lift trends, recovery, pain, adherence; avoid forcing load increases |
| 5–6 | Progress loads only when criteria met | All sets at top of rep range, appropriate RIR, no deterioration in form or symptoms |
| 7–8 | Review maintenance effectiveness | Weight trend, strength retention, fatigue, sleep/recovery and next-block decision |

### What counts as programme success?

- Body weight is broadly stable over a **2–3-week rolling average**, if weight maintenance is the goal.
- Core comparable lifts remain stable or improve without persistent loss of repetitions or load.
- Shoulder and joint symptoms are not worsening.
- Lower fatigue and acceptable recovery relative to the previous higher-volume approach.
- Regular LISS is achieved without it interfering with lifting performance.

**If lifts regress:** Before increasing training volume, check calories, protein, sleep, exercise identity, changes in machine setup, accumulated fatigue and shoulder limitations. A repeatable downward trend across comparable sessions may indicate insufficient volume, insufficient recovery or other factors; do not assume a single cause.

## 8. Maintenance-phase nutrition guide

| Variable | Starting guideline | How VANTAGE should evaluate |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Start around estimated maintenance calories | Review 2–3-week average body-weight trend; adjust to observed maintenance rather than relying only on calculated TDEE |
| Protein | **1.6–2.2 g/kg body weight/day** | Use current measured weight, not an old recalled weight; distribute intake to suit preferences |
| Carbohydrates | Adequate to support lifting and LISS | Monitor training performance and recovery; no fixed carb target without current calorie budget |
| Fat | Balance remaining calories; avoid unnecessarily low intake | Check total energy, dietary quality and satiety |
| Trial | Eight weeks | Review at weeks 2, 4, 6 and 8 |

**Diet note:** This phase targets maintenance, not a sustained cutting deficit. An 18–24-hour fast on non-training days is not a requirement of this programme; if practised, ensure overall calories and protein still support maintenance and recovery.

**Known food exclusions to respect in future meal examples:** Oats and sesame (allergies). Do not introduce these in generated meal plans.

## 9. VANTAGE Coach operating instructions

**Treat this document as an ACTIVE PROGRAMME TEMPLATE only after user activation/confirmation in the VANTAGE system. It is not evidence that a session was performed.**

On programme activation:

1. Use programme ID `VANTAGE-HDM-AB-LISS-2026-10` and preserve the schedule, exercise IDs and targets.
2. Generate planned workout cards for `HDM-A`, `HDM-B` and the four LISS day types. Alternate Weeks 1 and 2 for eight weeks from the user-selected start date.
3. Distinguish *planned* sets/reps from *performed* sets/reps; never silently mark planned activity complete.
4. Prefer observed VANTAGE app values for completed loads, repetitions, durations, exertion and recovery; state missing values as **not recorded**.
5. Analyse recovery/readiness before judging session performance, preserving source discrepancies rather than averaging incompatible values.
6. Track **working sets separately from warm-up sets**. Track compounds vs isolation work distinctly.
7. Compare only matching exercise and equipment identities over time. Substituted exercises start their own trend lines.
8. Preserve the difference between *recorded facts*, *calculations*, *coach interpretation* and *data-quality issues* in session summaries.
9. If chest-first and legs-first variants occur for `HDM-A`, record the variant because it changes fatigue conditions.
10. Flag persistent shoulder symptoms, disproportionate fatigue or falling strength as review items; do not automatically push sets to failure.
11. Record Sunday as either `easy LISS completed`, `complete rest` or `not logged`, as appropriate.
12. At weeks 2/4/6/8, generate a concise programme review with strength trends, adherence, LISS totals, symptoms, fatigue, recovery and next-step recommendations.

## 12. Evidence and limitations

- Heavy Duty's principles of hard, purposeful working sets and progressive overload are useful, but maximal failure on every set is **not necessary** to stimulate or maintain muscle.
- Lower training volumes can often maintain previously gained muscle and strength, provided the resistance stimulus remains sufficiently challenging and nutrition/recovery are adequate.
- The programme's initial set count is a **starting hypothesis**, not a guaranteed optimal volume. The eight-week VANTAGE trend review determines whether it is sufficient for this individual.
- LISS is deliberately low in intensity; more cardio is not automatically better if strength maintenance and recovery are the priorities.
- Persistent load-related shoulder pain warrants assessment rather than being masked by exercise substitutions indefinitely.
